Project 1

Education For All — Donor Retention & Giving Analysis

Tools Used: SQL (JOIN, GROUP BY, ORDER BY, LIMIT), Tableau

Project Description: A donor-analytics brief for Education For All (EFA), a UNESCO-led movement providing free education to underprivileged rural children. Donor demographic data and donation records arrived as two separate tables, joined in SQL on donor ID, to determine who gives, how often, and where the fundraising team should focus to grow donor count, donation frequency and donation value. Results were visualised in an interactive Tableau dashboard. The dashboard includes the following features:

  1. Total Donations Summary: total, average and count of donations grouped by frequency.
  2. Top 11 Donors: highest individual donations, colour-coded by frequency.
  3. Donation Frequency by Percentage: donut chart of monthly, once, weekly and yearly giving.
  4. Donation Rate by State: choropleth map of donation totals across all 50 US states.
  5. Donation Frequency Rate by Gender: bar chart comparing male and female giving by frequency.
  6. Donation Rate by Job Field: stacked bar chart across 12 job fields, split by gender.
  7. Donation Percentage by Gender: donut chart of overall donation value share.

Key findings

  1. One-time donors are nearly as valuable as yearly donors — "Once" gifts total $64,586 versus $65,667 from yearly donors, despite never being asked again. Converting even a fraction of one-time donors into repeat givers is the single biggest lever in the data.
  2. More women give, but men give more. Female donors outnumber male donors 508 to 492, yet male donors contribute a higher total ($127,628 vs $121,457) and a higher average gift ($259 vs $239).
  3. Donation value is geographically concentrated — California ($30,264), Texas ($24,097) and Florida ($20,562) together account for roughly a third of all donations across the 50 states tracked.

Recommendation

Prioritise retention outreach toward recurring donors — weekly, monthly and yearly givers, in that order — since they represent sustained rather than one-off support, and target donor acquisition toward the top-performing states rather than spreading effort evenly.

Project 2

Udemy Course Analysis

Tools Used: Microsoft Excel & Google Sheets (Pivot Tables, VLOOKUP, IF, LEFT/RIGHT functions), Tableau

Project Description: Four raw Udemy category exports — Business Finance, Graphic Design, Musical Instruments and Web Development — combined into one working dataset to identify the top 20 most-subscribed courses and understand where course-marketplace revenue opportunity actually lies. Course level and duration were pulled in with VLOOKUP, paid/free and free-beginner status derived with nested IF logic, and publish dates parsed out of raw text with LEFT/RIGHT. The cleaned model was rebuilt as an interactive Tableau dashboard. The dashboard includes the following features:

  1. Course Selector: interactive filter to isolate any single course across the full dataset.
  2. Top 20 Courses Table: ranked list of the most-subscribed courses with level and paid/free status.
  3. Top 5 Most-Subscribed Courses: pie-chart breakdown of the five highest-subscriber courses.
  4. Subscribers by Course: horizontal bar chart of all 20 top courses with publish dates.
  5. Courses by Duration: bar chart comparing course length across the top 20.
  6. Average Price by Subject: Web Development, Business Finance, Graphic Design and Musical Instruments compared.
  7. Average Rating by Subject: course ratings compared across the four subject categories.

Key findings

  1. Web Development dominates the market — 7.98M of the 11.76M subscribers analysed (68%) sit in Web Development courses, more than the other three subjects combined.
  2. Rating and price don't move together. Musical Instruments courses average the lowest rating (0.31) despite being the cheapest subject on average ($50); Graphic Design's expert-level courses rate highest of any level (0.88).
  3. Web Development also commands the highest average price ($75), yet still converts the most subscribers — the strongest signal of where a new course would find demand.

Conclusion

The clearest opportunity for new course investment is Web Development across all levels, followed by Business Finance — both combine high subscriber volume with pricing headroom, while Musical Instruments shows neither.
